Long-ferment sourdough

Sardo × Dave Pizza

Dave’s practical home-oven process, rebuilt around a ripe starter and a slow 48–72 hour cold ferment.

  • 2 × 14-inch pizzas
  • ~65% hydration
  • 48–72 hours cold
  • 6–8 min bake

Dough

≈ 750 g total
  • 280 gbread flour
  • 120 gall-purpose flour
  • 246 gcold water
  • 80 gripe sourdough starter 100% hydration; fed 4–8 hours before mixing and nicely doubled
  • 10 gfine sea salt
  • 8 golive oil
  • 8 gsugar Use 2 g instead in a pizza oven above 700°F

Flour note: the 400 g listed here is in addition to the flour already present in the starter.

~65% true hydration Including the flour and water in the starter

The method

Cold water, restrained mixing, folds for strength, and enough time for the dough to become flavorful and relaxed.

Day 1

Mix day

  1. Dissolve the starter in the cold water. Add both flours and mix to a shaggy mass. Cover and rest 45–60 minutes.
  2. Add the salt, sugar, and olive oil. Pinch and fold until incorporated. Deliberately under-mix here; the folds will finish the structure.
  3. Perform 3–4 sets of stretch-and-folds, 30 minutes apart.
  4. Cover and bulk ferment at room temperature until puffy and about 50% risen. In a warm kitchen, this may take 3–4 hours from mixing. Watch the dough, not the clock.
  5. Place the dough in a lightly oiled, covered container and refrigerate to begin the cold ferment.
Days 2–3

Cold ferment

  1. Keep the covered dough cold for a total refrigerated fermentation of 48–72 hours. The day before baking, divide it into two portions of about 375 g each, shape into tight balls, place seam-side down in lightly oiled containers, and return them to the fridge.
Pizza day

Warm, stretch & bake

  1. Pull the dough balls from the fridge 3–4 hours before baking and let them come fully to room temperature.
  2. Heat a pizza steel or stone at your oven’s maximum, ideally 500–550°F, for a full 45–60 minutes.
  3. On a floured surface, press from the center outward, leaving a puffy rim. Skip the rolling pin to protect the bubbles.
  4. Top lightly, then bake for 6–8 minutes, until the crust is deeply browned and blistered.
Thursday morning Mix & fold

Bulk until 50% risen, then cover and begin the cold ferment.

Friday Divide & ball

Shape two 375 g balls the day before baking, then return them to the fridge.

Saturday night Pizza

Warm the dough for 3–4 hours, stretch, top, and bake.

Why the sugar?

Dave uses it to promote browning in a home oven. Reduce it to 2 g when baking above 700°F, where the intense heat supplies plenty of color.

Starter waking up?

If it has been resting in the refrigerator, give it one or two feeds before mix day. Use it when it is active, doubled, and pleasantly aromatic.
